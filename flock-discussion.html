Shasta County Flock ALPR — The Public Discussion

Redacted public record. Private citizens’ names are removed; public officials’ names stay. Nothing here is a finding — it is the discussion itself, and the claims in it, laid out so the people can judge them.

1. The post that started it

November 10, 2026, 1:47 PM PST · Shared in a Shasta County community crime-watch group · Source: Shasta County Sheriff’s Office

The Shasta County Sheriff’s Office asked the community to email opinions on Flock cameras — after the cameras were already installed. The post shared Flock Safety’s explainer image, which states Flock reads license plates and vehicle attributes, and claims: no facial biometric analysis, no traffic citations, no tracking of individuals, no database; SCSO controls sharing; data deleted after 30 days.

Those claims come from Flock Safety’s own explainer and SCSO’s statements — a press release, not an independent finding. Every one of them is a claim to be verified.

2. The community response — 21 comments, overwhelmingly opposed

Themes from the public comments (summarized; commenters’ names redacted — they are private citizens)
  • Cameras installed without asking — SCSO only asks now because people are aware and angry.
  • The DA’s office has 1,000+ incidents unaddressed (a commenter’s claim, not a verified finding).
  • Fourth Amendment concerns raised by residents; some framed the cameras as communism.
  • Groups have been set up to locate and dismantle cameras.
  • One commenter referenced thecitizenmovement.com and the movement.
  • One commenter promised an editorial on “Zogg money.”
  • One commenter alleged Zogg fire fund money was used for Flock cameras instead of going to fire victims — unverified allegation, flagged for follow-up (see investigation 3, Zogg Fire Money).

3. The press-release criteria — what we are questioning

From Flock Safety’s explainer and SCSO’s statements
  • Flock reads license plates and vehicle attributes.
  • Flock states it does no facial biometric analysis.
  • Flock states it issues no traffic citations.
  • Flock states it does not track individuals.
  • Flock states there is no database.
  • SCSO states it controls sharing.
  • SCSO states data is deleted after 30 days.
That is why we are doing the poll: the people decide whether the cameras stay, not the press release. Vote on Flock cameras.
