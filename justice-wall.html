Case 001 — Henry William Lane

Red Bluff, California · April 14, 2019 · Red Bluff Police Department

Henry William Lane, 42, of Red Bluff, was shot and killed by RBPD Cpl. Stephen Harper just after midnight on April 14, 2019, on Antelope Boulevard just east of Rio Street. Per the official account, officers began life-saving measures until paramedics arrived; he was taken to St. Elizabeth Community Hospital, where he was declared dead.

Official record

Per the 2019 official account: a 911 call reported a man in mental-health crisis banging on a closed business and screaming that he wanted a gun. Officers later found Lane at a bus stop; four officers approached with guns and tasers drawn. Less-lethal attempts — a stun gun and bean-bag rounds — failed, and Harper fired one round, striking Lane in the upper body. The Tehama County District Attorney ruled the shooting legally justified (reported October 2020). Body-worn camera footage exists.

Family & witness accounts

Lane's family says he was harassed by police in the weeks leading up to his death. The family's Facebook page published an April 17, 2019 video containing the first 9 minutes 50 seconds of audio from 12:20 AM on April 14, audio from the earlier call that night, and audio from three weeks prior described as another unnecessary use of force. Radio logs covering the prior weeks were independently requested and posted by a private citizen — they do not appear in the department's own video release or press presentation, which covered the incident itself.

Why he is on the wall

The reviewing authority ruled from a record that omitted the weeks-long pattern preceding the shooting. The pattern evidence exists only because it was independently requested and published. A ruling built on an incomplete record is an incomplete ruling — and the public deserves to see what the file left out.

Status: Under public review. Family contact welcome via the contact card.

Case 002 — Douglas “Doug” Harless

Lily, Laurel County, Kentucky · December 23, 2024 · London Police Department

Douglas Harless, 63, was shot and killed in his own living room at 511 Vanzant Road, Lily, Kentucky, near midnight on December 23, 2024. London Police Department officers were executing a search warrant for stolen lawn equipment — a warrant written for 489 Vanzant Road. They raided 511. Awakened by intruders breaching his door, Harless stood in his living room holding a handgun. Detective Joshua Morgan fired five rifle rounds through the doorway, killing him. Harless never fired a shot. Round trajectories were recovered from the storm door, walls, refrigerator, and kitchen cabinets.

Official record

The Kentucky State Police investigative file — over 1,000 pages, released only after a court order following 14+ months withheld — established: dispatch audio has officers broadcasting the 489 address while raiding 511, realizing the error only after the shooting; “511” was posted on the house and lit by a motion light; officers claimed four sources had verified the address, and KSP tested all four — none supported it; the department’s mandatory address-verification policy was violated, with no operational plan and no supervisory oversight; and the informant, already in custody, never named Harless or his address.

Why he is on the wall

A midnight raid hit the wrong house. A 63-year-old man, awakened by his own door being breached, was killed in his living room without firing a shot — and the department’s own dispatch audio recorded the wrong address being broadcast as it happened. The file documenting all of it was withheld for over a year and released only by court order. On February 20, 2026, a grand jury returned a no-bill with a special prosecutor presenting; Morgan resigned after the records became public.

Status: Daughters Nicki Lawson and Mona Alsip filed a federal wrongful-death suit October 7, 2025 — Lawson v. London Police Department, 6:25-cv-00176 (E.D. Ky.), naming the City of London and 11 officers. On February 24, 2026, all Laurel County elected officials jointly requested an FBI/DOJ Civil Rights Division investigation; Governor Beshear backed federal review. Family contact welcome via the contact card.

The law, in theory

Title IV-D. Title IV-D of the Social Security Act, 42 U.S.C. §§ 651–669b, is the federal-state child support enforcement program. The federal government reimburses 66% of a state’s eligible administrative costs to run the program (42 U.S.C. § 655; 45 C.F.R. § 304.20) — reimbursement for the cost of running the program, not a share of the support collected, and not a payment to judges (45 C.F.R. § 304.21(b)). Incentive payments under 42 U.S.C. § 658a are separate, performance-based, capped, and must be reinvested in the program.

Trafficking. Human trafficking under federal law requires three elements — the act, the means, and the purpose (22 U.S.C. § 7102; 18 U.S.C. § 1591). Coercion is defined to include the abuse or threatened abuse of the legal process (22 U.S.C. § 7102(3)(C)). But trafficking still requires the purpose element — a commercial sex act or forced labor. False abuse allegations to win custody or obtain a support order do not induce a commercial sex act and do not put someone into forced labor. They are perjury, false reporting, fraud on the court, and abuse of process — real and actionable wrongs, a different legal category than trafficking.

The framework. In theory, the pattern maps to conspiracy against rights (18 U.S.C. § 241), deprivation of rights under color of law (18 U.S.C. § 242; civil action, 42 U.S.C. § 1983), malicious prosecution, abuse of process, false imprisonment, and fraud on the court. Knowledge is what makes the conduct willful: if officials knew the charges were false and proceeded anyway, that is not negligence.

The Thirteenth Amendment. “Neither slavery nor involuntary servitude, except as a punishment for crime whereof the party shall have been duly convicted, shall exist within the United States.” The exception turns on two words: “duly convicted.” A conviction built on allegations the officials knew were false is not duly obtained, and the punishment exception does not cover it. The Founder served as a certified wildland firefighter after completing the fire camp program, with real missions and fire calls. If a person provided labor to the state while held under a conviction the officials knew was false — including fire camp labor — that labor was extracted under a conviction that was never lawful. That is involuntary servitude. Involuntary servitude is slavery.

The audit criteria — violations tracked

Each item below is an audit question — who knew, who concealed, who failed to act — not a concluded finding. Whether any violation occurred turns on proof, and liability is a question for an attorney.

  • Misprision of a felony, 18 U.S.C. § 4. Whoever, having knowledge of the actual commission of a felony cognizable by a court of the United States, conceals and does not as soon as possible make known the same to some judge or other person in civil or military authority under the United States, is guilty of misprision of a felony — fine and/or up to three years. The audit question: who knew of the actual commission of a felony, who concealed it, and who failed to report it to a judge or other person in authority? This is the same statute behind the Mandate panel on the site (18 U.S.C. § 4).
  • Conspiracy against rights, 18 U.S.C. § 241. Who planned together to injure, oppress, or intimidate the Founder in the free exercise of a right secured by the Constitution?
  • Deprivation of rights under color of law, 18 U.S.C. § 242; 42 U.S.C. § 1983. Who, acting under color of law, willfully deprived him of liberty, property, or due process — including the denial of discovery and of counsel who would listen?
  • Malicious prosecution; abuse of process; false imprisonment; fraud on the court; perjury. Which proceedings were instituted without probable cause, for an improper purpose, on allegations known to be false?
  • Welfare fraud, Welf. & Inst. Code § 10980; federal SNAP fraud, 7 U.S.C. § 2024(b); false statements, 18 U.S.C. § 1001. Which false statements to a government agency obtained cash aid or food stamps to which the person was not entitled?
  • False report, Pen. Code § 148.5; stalking, Pen. Code § 646.9; criminal threats, Pen. Code § 422; conspiracy, Pen. Code § 182; theft, Pen. Code § 484; destroying or concealing evidence, Pen. Code § 135. Each act in the continuing pattern carries its own statute; each is tracked the same way — who did what, when, and what is the proof?
  • The Scott Bill. Zero immunity for any public official whose role is to protect citizens’ rights and who fails to do so — law enforcement, prosecutors, judges, child support enforcement officials, and others charged with safeguarding liberty and property. The bill addresses the accountability gap at the center of this case: under qualified immunity, officials who knew charges were false, who refused to act on a reported theft, who denied discovery, and who allowed a pattern of false detention to continue have avoided liability. The Scott Bill would remove that shield.

Case 006 — Justice for Nikki

Anderson, California (Shasta County) · Born November 9, 1984 · Missing since May 17, 2024 · Shasta County Sheriff’s Office Case No. SCSO24-002069

Nikki Cheng Orn Saelee-McCain, 39, a mother of four from Anderson, has been missing since May 17, 2024. Her body has never been found. In March 2025 her case was ruled a homicide — Shasta County’s first no-body murder case. Her husband is accused of her murder and is awaiting trial. He has pleaded not guilty and is presumed innocent until proven guilty. This case is on the wall because Nikki is still missing, and because the injustice is still happening now.

Nikki was 4 feet 11 inches tall and 110 pounds, an Asian female with brown hair and brown eyes, pierced ears, and a tattoo of roses on her left forearm. She may also use Chen as part of her name. Her date of birth, November 9, 1984, is grade B — it rests on birthday references (her family marked her 40th birthday on November 9, 2024, and a researcher’s post marks her 42nd this year), not on a certified record; the Charley Project lists a date-of-birth field but leaves it blank. It will be confirmed against a certified record when one is obtained.

Official record

Nikki suffered domestic violence in her marriage. In December 2023 she went to the emergency room after a prolonged assault and gave police a statement; her sister said her husband had also pointed a gun at her. He was charged with felony domestic violence and pleaded not guilty. Nikki was the key witness, due to testify. Her last contact was a text to her sisters at 12:35 a.m. on May 18, 2024, saying she was driving back to her mother-in-law’s property in Redding Rancheria to drop off her white Chevrolet Tahoe, and that one of her husband’s friends would follow to drive her home. That friend is named as existing but is not identified publicly. She was last seen at home on May 18, 2024, and was reported missing on May 22, 2024. On May 24, 2024 her gray 2002 Chevrolet Avalanche — disabled plate DP154UT — was found abandoned in western Tehama County with a decomposition smell and blood and DNA evidence. On July 5, 2024 the domestic violence case was dismissed because Nikki could not appear. On March 14, 2025 authorities searched their home and ruled Nikki a homicide victim.

She had a settlement conference on June 3, 2024, weeks after she vanished, in a divorce she had already planned. She had been looking at furniture for a new place with the kids, and her sister described her as really excited. Her four children had been removed from both parents in 2022, and her sisters now have custody.

Open lead — the red pickup

Investigators have asked for the driver of a red pickup that may have picked up an adult male near Highway 36, by R Wild Horse Ranch or the Beegum Gorge Bridge. That red truck is still an open lead in the public record. It is noted here as an open lead, not resolved.

The failure to protect

The accused faced four felonies from December 2023: domestic violence, assault, false imprisonment, and criminal threats. Nikki told police he bound her ankles and wrists, taped her mouth, and beat her for three hours, and that he intended to kill her. Her sister said she was so badly beaten she barely recognized her. On July 5, 2024 the district attorney’s office dismissed all four charges without prejudice, with the stated reason that Nikki, the sole witness, did not appear. She was dead. The sequence is the point: the case was dismissed because the victim could not testify, and the reason she could not testify is the crime being prosecuted. District Attorney Stephanie Bridgett’s public explanation was that the office could not proceed without the accused’s constitutional right to confront his accuser. A Redding legal expert went on camera about that dismissal.

The sequence, in order: he was released pending trial on the December 2023 charges, and a protective order was in place. After she disappeared he was arrested on July 30, 2024 for violating the protective order, with ammunition found, then posted bail and was released again. On April 1, 2025 a traffic stop found him carrying her partially burned driver’s license. Murder was not charged until August 20, 2025, fifteen months after she vanished. His defense attorney, Michael Borges, filed a motion to delay the trial and to move it out of Shasta County, saying it was unlikely a fair and impartial jury could be found in Shasta County. He has been held without bail since, was held to answer in September 2025, and trial is now set for February 23, 2027 (Judge Adam Ryan). The district attorney stated the motive was to prevent Nikki from testifying in the domestic abuse case.

The charge — presumed innocent

Her husband is accused of murder. He has pleaded not guilty, and the trial has not happened. Guilt has not been established. What is established is that Nikki is missing, that her case was ruled a homicide, that her husband is accused and held without bail awaiting trial, and that her body has never been found. The wall does not declare guilt. It declares that Nikki is owed justice — finding her, and accountability through the trial.

Why she is on the wall

A mother of four went to the emergency room, gave police a statement, and was the key witness in a domestic violence case — and then she disappeared. The case against her husband was dismissed because she could not appear. Ten months later her disappearance was ruled a homicide, and her husband was arrested for murder. Shasta County’s first no-body murder case is still open, her body has still not been found, and her trial is still more than a year away. The Founder states she was part of the tribe. The call is Justice for Nikki: find her, and hold the trial to account.
