How a case earns the wall

Not every officer-involved shooting or in-custody death is placed here. Placement follows a standard, applied the same way every time:

  • Excessive force or a deliberately overboard death or injury earns placement.
  • Cases are graded by verification: official record, independently verifiable material, and family or witness accounts are labeled for what they are.
  • The wall is not a court. It preserves the record — including what the official record left out — so the public, the press, attorneys, and investigators can see the whole picture.

The cases

Case 001 — Henry William Lane

Red Bluff, California · April 14, 2019 · Red Bluff Police Department

Henry William Lane, 42, of Red Bluff, was shot and killed by RBPD Cpl. Stephen Harper just after midnight on April 14, 2019, on Antelope Boulevard just east of Rio Street. He died at St. Elizabeth Community Hospital.

Official record

Per the 2019 official account: a 911 call reported a man in mental-health crisis banging on a closed business and screaming that he wanted a gun. Officers later found Lane at a bus stop; four officers approached with guns and tasers drawn. Less-lethal attempts — a stun gun and bean-bag rounds — failed, and Harper fired one round, striking Lane in the upper body. The Tehama County District Attorney ruled the shooting legally justified (reported October 2020). Body-worn camera footage exists.

Family & witness accounts

Lane's family says he was harassed by police in the weeks leading up to his death. The family's Facebook page (justice.forhenry.37) published an April 17, 2019 video containing the first 9 minutes 50 seconds of audio from 12:20 AM on April 14, audio from the earlier call that night, and audio from three weeks prior described as another unnecessary use of force. Radio logs covering the prior weeks were independently requested and posted by a private citizen — they do not appear in the department's own video release or press presentation, which covered the incident itself.

Why it is on the wall

The reviewing authority ruled from a record that omitted the weeks-long pattern preceding the shooting. The pattern evidence exists only because it was independently requested and published. A ruling built on an incomplete record is an incomplete ruling — and the public deserves to see what the file left out.

Status: Under public review. Family contact welcome via the contact card.
