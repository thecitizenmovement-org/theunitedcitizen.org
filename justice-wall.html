Case 001 — Henry William Lane

Red Bluff, California · April 14, 2019 · Red Bluff Police Department

Henry William Lane, 42, of Red Bluff, was shot and killed by RBPD Cpl. Stephen Harper just after midnight on April 14, 2019, on Antelope Boulevard just east of Rio Street. Per the official account, officers began life-saving measures until paramedics arrived; he was taken to St. Elizabeth Community Hospital, where he was declared dead.

Official record

Per the 2019 official account: a 911 call reported a man in mental-health crisis banging on a closed business and screaming that he wanted a gun. Officers later found Lane at a bus stop; four officers approached with guns and tasers drawn. Less-lethal attempts — a stun gun and bean-bag rounds — failed, and Harper fired one round, striking Lane in the upper body. The Tehama County District Attorney ruled the shooting legally justified (reported October 2020). Body-worn camera footage exists.

Family & witness accounts

Lane's family says he was harassed by police in the weeks leading up to his death. The family's Facebook page published an April 17, 2019 video containing the first 9 minutes 50 seconds of audio from 12:20 AM on April 14, audio from the earlier call that night, and audio from three weeks prior described as another unnecessary use of force. Radio logs covering the prior weeks were independently requested and posted by a private citizen — they do not appear in the department's own video release or press presentation, which covered the incident itself.

Why he is on the wall

The reviewing authority ruled from a record that omitted the weeks-long pattern preceding the shooting. The pattern evidence exists only because it was independently requested and published. A ruling built on an incomplete record is an incomplete ruling — and the public deserves to see what the file left out.

Status: Under public review. Family contact welcome via the contact card.

Case 002 — Douglas “Doug” Harless

Lily, Laurel County, Kentucky · December 23, 2024 · London Police Department

Douglas Harless, 63, was shot and killed in his own living room at 511 Vanzant Road, Lily, Kentucky, near midnight on December 23, 2024. London Police Department officers were executing a search warrant for stolen lawn equipment — a warrant written for 489 Vanzant Road. They raided 511. Awakened by intruders breaching his door, Harless stood in his living room holding a handgun. Detective Joshua Morgan fired five rifle rounds through the doorway, killing him. Harless never fired a shot. Round trajectories were recovered from the storm door, walls, refrigerator, and kitchen cabinets.

Official record

The Kentucky State Police investigative file — over 1,000 pages, released only after a court order following 14+ months withheld — established: dispatch audio has officers broadcasting the 489 address while raiding 511, realizing the error only after the shooting; “511” was posted on the house and lit by a motion light; officers claimed four sources had verified the address, and KSP tested all four — none supported it; the department’s mandatory address-verification policy was violated, with no operational plan and no supervisory oversight; and the informant, already in custody, never named Harless or his address.

Why he is on the wall

A midnight raid hit the wrong house. A 63-year-old man, awakened by his own door being breached, was killed in his living room without firing a shot — and the department’s own dispatch audio recorded the wrong address being broadcast as it happened. The file documenting all of it was withheld for over a year and released only by court order. On February 20, 2026, a grand jury returned a no-bill with a special prosecutor presenting; Morgan resigned after the records became public.

Status: Daughters Nicki Lawson and Mona Alsip filed a federal wrongful-death suit October 7, 2025 — Lawson v. London Police Department, 6:25-cv-00176 (E.D. Ky.), naming the City of London and 11 officers. On February 24, 2026, all Laurel County elected officials jointly requested an FBI/DOJ Civil Rights Division investigation; Governor Beshear backed federal review. Family contact welcome via the contact card.

Sources: WKYT (wrong-home report), FOX 56 (case file), WTVQ (records report), CourtListener (docket).
