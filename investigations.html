2. Shasta County Flock ALPR Investigation

Shasta County Sheriff's Office Flock license-plate cameras — installed first, public comment asked for after.

  • SCSO asked the community to email opinions on Flock cameras on November 10, 2026 — after the cameras were already in place.
  • The community response was overwhelmingly opposed: cameras installed without asking, the DA's office with 1,000+ incidents unaddressed (a commenter's claim), and Fourth Amendment concerns raised by residents.
  • Flock reads license plates and vehicle attributes. Flock states it does no facial biometric analysis, issues no traffic citations, and does not track individuals. SCSO states it controls sharing and deletes data after 30 days.

4. Justice for Nikki — Shasta County’s First No-Body Murder Case

Nikki Cheng Orn Saelee-McCain, 39, a mother of four from Anderson, has been missing since May 17, 2024. Her body has never been found. In March 2025 her case was ruled a homicide — Shasta County’s first no-body murder case. Her husband is accused of her murder, has pleaded not guilty, and is held without bail. Trial is set for February 23, 2027.

  • In December 2023 Nikki went to the emergency room after a prolonged assault and gave police a statement. Her husband was charged with four felonies. Nikki was the key witness, due to testify.
  • On July 5, 2024 the district attorney’s office dismissed all four charges because Nikki, the sole witness, did not appear. She was dead.
  • Her gray 2002 Chevrolet Avalanche was found abandoned in western Tehama County on May 24/25, 2024, with blood and DNA evidence. A red pickup seen near Highway 36 remains an open lead.
  • At the September 17, 2025 preliminary hearing, a witness identified in news coverage reportedly recanted an earlier account in which he said the accused had confessed. The same coverage reports the prosecution played recordings of his earlier statements. These are newspaper accounts of testimony, not a transcript or a court finding. The judge held the accused to answer on all charges on September 23, 2025.
The failure to protect: the case was dismissed because the victim could not testify — and the reason she could not testify is the crime being prosecuted. Two documents are outstanding: the July 5, 2024 dismissal minute order with the stated reason, and the protective order compliance history showing the two releases.

5. Redding Police Chief Brian Barner — Investigation, Leave, and Reinstatement

Brian Barner was the Redding Police Chief. An administrative investigation was opened, he was placed on leave, and he was reinstated within a week. No formal reprimand or discipline was ever publicly announced, and no criminal charges were filed. He retired with 34 years of service.

  • The investigation notice listed four administrative and policy items: personal use of a department trailer (duck hunting); off-the-books time off; personal gifts for staff without authority (baby onesies, whiskey glasses, cutting boards); raffle prizes involving department weapons and SWAT equipment.
  • The leave was reported, and the January 6 council meeting drew 40+ minutes of public comment. What stayed non-public was the substance and the findings, classified as a “personnel matter” (Penal Code §832.7 / Government Code §6254(c)). The interim city manager decided alone; the January 6 closed session reported “no reportable action”; reinstatement was announced through the mayor’s Facebook post. No findings were ever published.
  • His pension was never at risk: Government Code §7522.72 (PEPRA) forfeiture requires a felony conviction for conduct in official duties, and he was never charged or convicted. He retired on a CalPERS safety retirement. 2024 total pay and benefits $372,801; 2025 $507,934 (includes lump-sum leave payouts). His exact monthly pension is not in public sources.
  • Interim chief during the leave: Robert “Hank” Schreeder.
What is not linked here: the Wynhoff lawsuit (Case No. 24CV-0204924) has no official confirmation tying it to Barner and is not cited. Barner is a defendant in Butler v. County of Shasta, No. 2:25-cv-01339 (E.D. Cal.); dismissal without leave to amend was recommended March 18, 2026 — that is not a finding of wrongdoing.

7. Robert Gibbs — Retaliation and the Courts

Robert A. Gibbs, publisher of shastacountywars.com, alleges that Shasta County and State of California officials subjected him to a continuous course of retaliation from 2013 to the present, after he filed civil-rights lawsuits against them. These are his allegations, stated as his account — not findings.

  • Warrantless entry and arrest by Fish and Wildlife officers in 2013; a false felon-in-possession charge by Deputy Chris Edwards, dismissed at arraignment; a multi-year jail term for speech on a private phone call in September 2015; years of solitary confinement and abuse in the Shasta County jail, with grievances, medical care, attorney access, and visits with his infant daughter denied.
  • Retaliatory prosecution by District Attorney Stephanie Bridgett, including a coerced guilty plea; and the removal of his infant daughter by a parole agent.
  • His filed federal case for the jail claims is Gibbs v. Webb, E.D. Cal. No. 2:18-cv-2817. He asks for investigation by an agency outside Shasta County and preservation of all records.
  • The Board of Supervisors was given notice of the jail-abuse allegations in August 2023 and again in February 2024. The Board’s own record supports the notice: its minutes for August 1, 2023 list Gibbs speaking during public comment with no Board action in response; on August 29, 2023 the agenda listed “Robert A. Gibbs v. J. Webb, et al.” under closed-session existing litigation.
  • The same people Gibbs accuses have since climbed the ranks: Cody Jones, now Cody Stenderup — Deputy District Attorney at the time, now Court Executive Officer and Clerk of the Shasta County Superior Court (since May 5, 2025); Ryan M. Birss — Gibbs’s own attorney at the time, now a Judge of the Shasta County Superior Court (appointed February 2, 2026, sworn in April 17, 2026).
Still employed: Stephanie A. Bridgett — District Attorney then, District Attorney now. Michael L. Johnson — Sheriff, appointed July 27, 2021, in office now.

8. Registrar of Voters Clint Curtis — Elections Under Scrutiny

Clint Curtis is the Shasta County Clerk and Registrar of Voters (outgoing; his term ends January 2027). He was appointed in early 2025 to finish the retiring registrar’s term, then lost the June 2, 2026 primary to Joanna Francescut, 42.03% to 57.97%. Francescut takes over in January 2027.

  • On September 9, 2026, the UCLA Voting Rights Project asked the Shasta County grand jury to initiate proceedings to remove Curtis from office, citing a pattern of conduct it says undermined the lawful and transparent administration of elections.
  • Curtis certified the June 2026 primary results, then raised unsubstantiated concerns about the ballots from the contest he administered and lost. He sealed ballots from the June 2026 primary and the November 2024 general election inside the county elections office.
  • The Board of Supervisors formally censured Curtis on August 11, 2026, for threats, verbal abuse, and retaliatory acts against employees (as reported).
  • November 2024 election materials were destroyed on Saturday, October 3, 2026, before noon, under Curtis’s direction and supervision via Hemsted Records Management of Redding — after the September 5 statutory deadline. The county CEO’s office issued a formal rebuke citing Curtis as “in violation” of state law, and Secretary of State Shirley Weber sent a direct compliance directive on September 30. Protesters and sheriff’s deputies were on scene during the October 3 shred. Curtis had halted a September 30 shred after finding election logs inside seven of the boxes; those logs are part of a public-records request (PRA #26-1766).
  • On August 25, 2026, a citizens’ press event outside the Sheriff’s Office demanded investigation into alleged election irregularities at the Registrar of Voters; the Sheriff’s Office said the FBI is investigating.
  • Curtis received a federal grand jury subpoena on September 10, 2026. The First Amendment Coalition sued Shasta County for its release (North State Community Journalism v. County of Shasta).
  • Election night, November 2026: overflowing ballot boxes, staffing shortages, misdirected ballots, computer malfunctions, and voters turned away by last-minute polling-place location changes, as documented by Shasta Scout. Curtis removed most of the county’s ballot drop boxes last year, citing security concerns.
Attribution: the removal request, the censure, the rebuke, and the destruction are reported by the sources named; the fraud claims Curtis raised about his own election are described in that reporting as unsubstantiated. Nothing here is a finding of guilt.

9. The District Attorney Connection — One Office, Three Cases

Stephanie Bridgett is the Shasta County District Attorney. Her office touches three of the cases on this page — each in a different way, each documented. The connection is the office, not a conclusion. Each case stands on its own record.

  • The Founder’s stolen Harley (investigation 6): the theft report RPD25-013423-1 was never forwarded to the DA. Kevin Skerba, DA Victim Advocate, confirmed in writing on October 12, 2026: no DA record, no submission from RPD. The DA’s office never received it.
  • Robert Gibbs (investigation 7): Gibbs alleges retaliatory prosecution by Bridgett, including a coerced guilty plea. That is Gibbs’s allegation, stated as his account — not a finding.
  • Nikki (investigation 4): Bridgett’s office dismissed all four domestic-violence charges on July 5, 2024, because Nikki, the sole witness, did not appear. She was dead. Bridgett’s public explanation: the office could not proceed without the accused’s constitutional right to confront his accuser.
Why this matters: one office, three cases, three different outcomes — a report never received, a prosecution alleged to be retaliatory, and a prosecution dismissed when the victim could not testify. The pattern is the question. The answer is in the records.

11. Justice for Henry Lane — More Coming Soon

Henry William Lane, 42, of Red Bluff, was shot and killed by RBPD Cpl. Stephen Harper just after midnight on April 14, 2019, on Antelope Boulevard just east of Rio Street. A 911 call reported a man in mental-health crisis. Less-lethal attempts — a stun gun and bean-bag rounds — failed, and Harper fired one round. The Tehama County District Attorney ruled the shooting legally justified (reported October 2020). Body-worn camera footage exists.

  • Lane’s family says he was harassed by police in the weeks leading up to his death. The family’s Facebook page published audio from that night and from three weeks prior, described as another unnecessary use of force.
  • Radio logs covering the prior weeks were independently requested and posted by a private citizen — they do not appear in the department’s own video release or press presentation, which covered the incident itself.
  • The reviewing authority ruled from a record that omitted the weeks-long pattern preceding the shooting. A ruling built on an incomplete record is an incomplete ruling.
More coming soon. The full case file is being assembled.
