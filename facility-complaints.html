FC-001 · Active Complaint

FCC Butner — Butner, North Carolina

Federal Correctional Complex · U.S. District Court jurisdiction: Eastern District of North Carolina

FacilitiesFCI Butner Medium I, FCI Butner Medium II, FCI Butner Low, FMC Butner (federal medical center), satellite prison camp
OperatorFederal Bureau of Prisons
PopulationRoughly 5,000 men. FMC Butner is the Bureau's largest medical complex — the system's sickest people are sent here, which is why its medical findings carry system-wide weight.
StatusACTIVE — under complaint

The death record

NPR's analysis of Bureau of Prisons records (September 2023) counted roughly 4,950 deaths in federal custody over about a decade — and about one in four of them, on the order of 1,200, occurred at Butner alone, one complex among more than 120 federal facilities. Documented individual cases include a man who died after staff failed to give him anti-epileptic medication, and two men who died overnight after not receiving timely medical attention. Current and former Butner staff told NPR the main driver was understaffing. This wall publishes the share documented by NPR; the Bureau does not publish a per-capita death rate for the complex, and none is claimed here.

Findings

F-B1 · Medical care the Bureau cannot measure

DOJ Office of the Inspector General, Audit 22-052 (March 16, 2022): the Bureau had no reliable, consistent process to evaluate the timeliness or quality of inmate healthcare at Butner, and transport failures for off-site appointments routinely delayed care. Fifteen recommendations were issued; by March 2023 only six were closed — nine remained open.

Inspection ask: the current status of all 15 recommendations, and the wait-time data behind them.

F-B2 · Kitchen sanitation — partially substantiated by the Bureau's own investigators

A Cook Supervisor's whistleblower disclosure, referred by the U.S. Office of Special Counsel to the President (June 4, 2025): FCI II kitchen roof leaks, standing water, damaged ceiling tiles, rainwater through light fixtures, apparent mold exposing staff and inmates; meals stored at improper temperatures; expired food served; routine unapproved deviations from the National Meal Plan; birds in food areas. The Bureau's Office of Internal Affairs partially substantiated the allegations.

Inspection ask: kitchen temperature logs, meal-plan deviation approvals, roof repair records.

F-B3 · Water reported undrinkable

Families of men held at Butner report brown, undrinkable water — echoed independently by former inmates ("brown as coffee"; brown water at the Low; mold, broken showers, and unusable water in the medical cadre workers' unit) in October 2026 reporting by Aaron Peila of the prisons and parole beat.

Inspection ask: water-quality test results for each housing unit, produced for the record.

F-B4 · Release processing delays

Case managers stalling First Step Act credits and halfway-house / home-confinement dates, reported from multiple families (Peila, October 2026; described as Bureau-systemic).

Inspection ask: the First Step Act credit application backlog, by unit.

What is being asked

Re-inspection by the DOJ Office of the Inspector General and the Bureau's Office of Internal Affairs against findings F-B1 through F-B4; a pattern-or-practice investigation by the DOJ Civil Rights Division under the Civil Rights of Institutionalized Persons Act; and judicial oversight in the Eastern District of North Carolina — up to a special master and closure evaluation, on the FCI Dublin model (FC-003) — if the complex cannot be brought to standard.

For families

Formal channels: the Bureau's Administrative Remedy Program (28 C.F.R. Part 542) — institution, regional office, Central Office; the DOJ Office of the Inspector General hotline (oig.justice.gov/hotline); congressional casework through the North Carolina delegation; the ACLU National Prison Project. Document every step — outside reviewers ask for it first.

Sources: DOJ OIG Audit 22-052 (Mar 16, 2022); NPR investigation, "1 in 4 inmate deaths happens in the same federal prison" (Sept 23, 2023); U.S. Office of Special Counsel §1213 whistleblower referral (transmitted June 4, 2025); Aaron Peila, "The issues at Butner federal prison complex, part 1" (Oct 13, 2026) and family confirmations in its comments. A reported recent stabbing death at Butner could not be independently confirmed in public records as of this writing and is not presented as fact.

FC-002 · Active Complaint

MDC Brooklyn — Brooklyn, New York

Metropolitan Detention Center · U.S. District Court jurisdiction: Eastern District of New York

FacilityFederal detention center, 80 29th Street, Sunset Park, Brooklyn — the only federal jail inside New York City
OperatorFederal Bureau of Prisons
PopulationRoughly 1,100–1,700 people — mostly pretrial detainees: people held awaiting trial, not convicted of the charge they are held on
StatusACTIVE — under complaint. Federal judges have repeatedly refused to send defendants here, calling conditions "dreadful."

The death & violence record

June 2020 — Jamel Floyd died after being pepper-sprayed by staff. The DOJ Inspector General conducted a formal investigation into his death. June 7, 2024 — Uriel Whyte, 37, held pretrial, was stabbed to death. July 17, 2024 — Edwin Cordero was stabbed to death by three fellow inmates with makeshift weapons: two homicides in six weeks. February 22, 2025 — a unit-wide melee left at least ten inmates stabbed or slashed. September 6, 2026 — a gang brawl involving some twenty inmates left several stabbed and slashed, followed by days of lockdown. In October 2026 a correctional officer at the facility was indicted by a federal grand jury for allegedly smuggling contraband for cash over seventeen months (charges are allegations).

Findings

F-M1 · A known, documented temperature-control failure

The Inspector General's inspection (September 26, 2019) of the January–February 2019 power and heat crisis — some 1,700 detainees in cold, dark cells — found longstanding temperature-regulation failures had already driven housing units to 59°F before the outage, and that the building lacked the equipment to continuously monitor temperatures.

Inspection ask: current temperature-monitoring capability and logs — the 2019 defect, verified fixed or not.

F-M2 · Violence the Bureau answered with an "urgent action team"

In October 2024 the Bureau formed an urgent action team for MDC Brooklyn and ran an interagency contraband sweep, naming staffing shortfalls and contraband itself. The murders, the mass-casualty melee, and the gang brawl above surround that intervention inside twenty-seven months.

Inspection ask: staffing ratios by shift against authorized levels; contraband interdiction results since the sweep.

F-M3 · Staff integrity

A serving correctional officer stands indicted for an alleged contraband-for-cash scheme run in part through a contraband phone (DOJ, Eastern District of New York, October 2026).

Inspection ask: the results of the post-indictment integrity review, if one exists.

What is being asked

Re-inspection by the DOJ Office of the Inspector General and the Bureau's Office of Internal Affairs against findings F-M1 through F-M3; a pattern-or-practice investigation by the DOJ Civil Rights Division under the Civil Rights of Institutionalized Persons Act; and judicial oversight in the Eastern District of New York — up to a special master and closure evaluation, on the FCI Dublin model (FC-003) — if the jail cannot be brought to standard.

For families

Formal channels: the Bureau's Administrative Remedy Program (28 C.F.R. Part 542); the DOJ Office of the Inspector General hotline (oig.justice.gov/hotline); congressional casework through the New York delegation; the Federal Defenders of New York, who sued over the 2019 crisis and continue to litigate conditions. Document every step — outside reviewers ask for it first.

Sources: DOJ OIG inspection of the 2019 power/heat crisis (Sept 26, 2019); DOJ OIG Report of Investigation into the death of Jamel Floyd (listed in the DOJ 2024 Chief FOIA Officer report); DOJ, U.S. Attorney's Office for the Eastern District of New York — charges in the Whyte murder and the March 2025 charging sweep (incl. the February 22, 2025 melee), the Cordero murder indictment (24-CR-380), and the October 2026 correctional-officer bribery indictment; New York Daily News reporting on the September 6, 2026 brawl (BOP confirmed).

FC-003 · Deactivated — Closed · Memorial Listing

FCI Dublin — Dublin, California

Federal Correctional Institution · closed April 15, 2024

FacilityFederal Correctional Institution, Dublin (FCI Dublin) + satellite prison camp
Location5701 8th Street, Dublin, California (Alameda County, ~20 miles southeast of Oakland)
TypeLow-security federal prison for women, with a minimum-security camp
OperatorFederal Bureau of Prisons
Opened1974 (women-only from 2012)
Population at closure726 (115 in the camp)
ClosedApril 15, 2024 — last women transferred out by May 1, 2024
StatusDEACTIVATED. The Bureau's own 2026 analysis recommends it never reopen.

What happened there

For years, staff at FCI Dublin sexually abused the women held there — a culture so open that inmates and staff alike called the prison "the rape club." The warden himself, Ray Garcia, was charged in 2021 with sexually assaulting an incarcerated woman and keeping nude photos of victims on his work computer. In the end, nine correctional officers, including the warden, were convicted. Survivors said the abuse had run for decades while warnings were ignored.

How it ended — the Dublin precedent

August 16, 2023 — the California Coalition for Women Prisoners filed a class action: CCWP v. BOP, No. 4:23-cv-04155-YGR, U.S. District Court, Northern District of California. January 2024 — after a 60 Minutes exposé, the FBI raided the prison and its leadership was removed; Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers held evidentiary hearings and made an unannounced, nine-hour inspection of the facility herself. March 15, 2024 — the court certified the class and ordered a special master — the first ever imposed on a federal prison. April 2024 — with the special master watching, the Bureau announced closure; the court supervised the transfers, found the Bureau's closure plan "ill-conceived," and ordered weekly status reports on every woman moved. February 25, 2025 — final approval of a consent decree with independent monitoring (at least two years), and a $116 million settlement paid to more than 100 survivors.

The watch — why this listing stays up

The Bureau's own September 2026 report concludes the facility should be deactivated — repairs would run about $118 million over ten years — and notes that of some 7,500 public comments on the closure, virtually all opposed ever using it to hold people again. When federal officials floated converting it to immigration detention, the Dublin City Council and the Alameda County Board of Supervisors passed resolutions in March 2026 opposing any reopening as a place of incarceration. This listing stands guard with them.

For the survivors

To the women who were held at FCI Dublin: this page is kept so the country cannot pretend your prison was ordinary, or that what happened to you was your fault, or that it did not happen at all. A judge walked your halls. The record is permanent. You survived a terrible place — and the place, not you, is the one that closed.

Sources: Court record, CCWP v. BOP, No. 4:23-cv-04155-YGR (N.D. Cal.) — class certification & special master order (Mar 15, 2024), closure orders (Apr–May 2024), consent decree final approval (Feb 25, 2025); KTVU reporting on the Bureau's deactivation report (Sept 2, 2026, signed by BOP Director William K. Marshall III); facility specifications cross-checked against the court timeline. Photo: Jesstess87, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons (commons.wikimedia.org/w/index.php?curid=90796483).

FC-004 · Active Complaint

Idaho County Jail — Grangeville, Idaho

Idaho County Sheriff’s Office — Jail Division · U.S. District Court jurisdiction: District of Idaho

FacilityIdaho County Jail (Idaho County Detention Center), 141 Airport Rd., Grangeville, Idaho
OperatorIdaho County Sheriff’s Office (Sheriff Doug Ulmer; Lt. Richard Knutson, Chief Jailer)
PopulationCounty jail. The detainee at the center of this complaint was held here, on his mother’s account, from September 25, 2025 until his transfer out on November 3, 2026. He is no longer on Idaho County’s current list, which fits the transfer out.
StatusACTIVE — under complaint

Why this facility is listed

This listing rests on the paper trail, not on a finding. Four U.S. Department of Justice Civil Rights Division complaints were filed on November 11, 2026, with record numbers 866855-RWJ, 866863-RTC, 866242-MHH, and 866841-QGK; three Idaho State Bar grievances were written and dated the same day; an FBI tip was submitted the same evening. Two of the four DOJ complaints name this jail. The grievances name, as they appear in the documents, Anne Taylor (the detainee’s attorney of record), Prosecutor Kirk Angus MacGregor, and Deputy Prosecutor Matt Jessup; the Taylor grievance also names District Judge Adam Green and Deputy Prosecutor Ann Kelleher in connection with its allegations. A grievance is an allegation, not a finding. The record numbers prove the submissions happened. They do not prove the allegations inside them. There is no inspector-general audit of this jail in hand, no court finding, and no inspection report in the package — what would verify or close each item is listed under the findings below. The criteria met: alleged threats and retaliation against a detainee for reporting to federal oversight; alleged blocking of legal mail and medical records; alleged denial of required care to a disabled pre-trial detainee; and an alleged pattern of complaints and inspections that went nowhere, with no punishment. This entry is an investigative alert, not a finding and not a court conclusion.

The account

The following is the account of ********** ********* (private person), the mother and legal power of attorney for ******* ** ***** *** (private person), her son, a pre-trial detainee described in the filings as 100% disabled. It is her account, tier C throughout unless a second source is noted. It is not a finding that the events occurred as described.

On October 9, 2026, Lt. Richard Knutson entered her son’s cell in the Idaho County Detention Center, directly threatened his life and safety, and ordered him to tell his mother to stop contacting the DOJ and outside oversight, warning “it was not going to end well for either one of us.” Multiple inmates witnessed the threat; recorded jail phone calls document witness statements; administrative kites requested an outside investigator and the Idaho State Police. The day after the threat, Knutson personally served her son’s dinner, arms crossed, smiling. Up to the November 3, 2026 transfer, officers intimidated him outside his cell, mocking him as “gimp.” Prosecutor Matt Jessup and the Sheriff failed to investigate. After repeated formal requests for complete medical records and HIPAA release documentation, her son was transferred about four hours away to Kootenai County Jail. The transfer itself is tier B — the Kootenai County incarceration record independently confirms it (November 3, 2026, 11:39 AM; “Hold for Other Agency”; arresting officer B. Whipple; arrest location Idaho County Jail; related case 2611HLD; $500,000 bond). The purpose of the transfer is tier C. On November 5, 2026, the mother sent a demand for immediate protective custody by external agencies and an emergency fax to the Kootenai command staff; on November 9, 2026, she sent a three-page fax to Sheriff Bob Norris, the DOJ Civil Rights Division (Special Litigation Section), and Disability Rights Idaho. Those documents exist and were read; their contents are her account.

Findings

F-I1 · The October 9 threat — tier C

Lt. Knutson entered the detainee’s cell and threatened his life and safety, ordering him to stop his mother’s reporting to the DOJ and other oversight agencies. The threat is the central incident in all four DOJ complaints and in two of the Bar grievances. Witnesses named in the filings: multiple inmates, with recorded phone-call statements; administrative kites requesting the Idaho State Police.

Inspection ask: jail video from the cell block for October 9, 2026; the administrative kites requesting outside investigation; the recorded inmate phone calls with witness statements; statements from the inmate witnesses themselves.

F-I2 · Disability harassment and denied care — tier C

Officers mocked the detainee as “gimp” outside his cell. The complaint alleges no ADA policies in the jail and a failure to provide required healthcare to a disabled pre-trial detainee, including acute renal failure twice in Idaho County custody. The medical neglect reaches back further than the fall: in a public post dated March 25, 2026, she states he was already suffering a systemic infection with red streaks and loss of hand function while held here, with medical transport denied — and she states the denials were logged under DOJ Case #746168-NMN, a number referenced in that post and not in the November filing log. The detainee is described in the filings as 100% disabled. No medical record is in hand, for the March episode or for the later care.

Inspection ask: the jail’s ADA policies and staff training records; the detainee’s medical records and sick-call / medical-request logs for the period held here, including the March 2026 episode described in her public post; any grievance or kite he filed about care.

F-I3 · Mail routed through the prosecutor’s office — tier C

The MacGregor grievance alleges the prosecutor issued unauthorized directives to jail administration requiring all of the detainee’s incoming and outgoing legal and personal mail to be routed through and explicitly approved by the prosecutor’s office before delivery — and that through this interception scheme he was denied access to incoming HIPAA medical release forms. The jail’s public position, per a separate post, is that personal mail is handled by jail staff and the Sheriff’s Office and never via the prosecutor, and that legal mail gets special handling. Those two statements do not contradict each other on their face: the jail’s position describes general practice; the grievance alleges a specific directive for one inmate. The movement’s Idaho jail-mail poll, live November 11, 2026, asks members exactly this question — “Should Idaho County Jail ever route an inmate’s personal mail through the Prosecuting Attorney’s office before it reaches the inmate?” The poll is a question to the members, not evidence of the answer.

Inspection ask: the jail’s written mail policy; any written directive concerning this detainee’s mail; the handling record for his incoming and outgoing mail.

F-I4 · Records blocked — tier C

The filings allege executed HIPAA release documentation was concealed and a properly executed, notarized medical power of attorney submitted October 13 was formally denied and obstructed by Deputy Prosecutor Matt Jessup, who asserted that because the detainee allegedly executed the document, he must request his records independently. The request sought medical documentation, jail video recordings, and photographic evidence from September 27 onward. The POA document itself is not in the package. Honest-law note: HIPAA has no private right of action; it is enforced by the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services. The enforceable claims for the same conduct run through the ADA, the civil rights statutes, and the Constitution — not through HIPAA as a standalone private claim.

Inspection ask: the executed HIPAA release form; the notarized medical power of attorney dated October 13; any written denial from the prosecutor’s office or the jail; the jail’s records-release log.

F-I5 · No investigation, no corrective action — and the retaliatory transfer — tier C

The filings allege no investigation of Knutson, no isolation of the officer, and zero corrective action by the Sheriff or the prosecutor, despite kites and direct contact requesting an outside investigator. The Jessup grievance further alleges administrative fabrication — backdated paperwork by jail personnel, specifically Sgt. Steven Gabor — in the period after the October 9 threat. The transfer to Kootenai County, about four hours away, is alleged to have followed the repeated records requests and to have been punishment for First Amendment activity — reporting to federal oversight. The transfer itself is tier B (independently confirmed, see the account above); its purpose is tier C. The retaliation account has a further chapter: in a public post dated June 22, 2026, she states Idaho County opened criminal case CR25-26-1001 against her on June 4, 2026 — three felony counts of witness intimidation and one count of improper influence — which she presents as retaliation for the tort claims and for speaking out. The case itself has not been independently verified, and the post does not name the person she is accused of intimidating. Two investigators have reported finding disturbing material about the facility; it has not yet been received and is not relied on here.

Inspection ask: internal investigation records and complaint logs concerning Lt. Knutson; the transfer authorization and the reason recorded for it; the disposition of every prior complaint against this jail in the period.

F-I6 · The pattern — years of complaints and inspections that went nowhere — tier C

The founder’s finding, in his words: “Years of no punishment, years of documented facility complaint issues and inspections that didn’t go nowhere — they went nowhere.” That is the pattern this complaint is built on, and it is tier C — his account and finding, not independently verified. What the record shows so far: formal reports to the Idaho Attorney General, the Governor’s office, state representatives, Idaho POST, and the DOJ are claimed in the November 5 fax; the November 5 requests for emergency protective custody, a welfare check, and an independent medical evaluation stand on the record as unanswered; and the filings allege no investigation and no corrective action after the October 9 threat. What would prove or disprove the pattern is the paper: the complaint logs, the inspection reports, and the disposition of each.

Inspection ask: the jail’s complaint log and every inspection report for the past five years, with the disposition of each finding; the record of any disciplinary action arising from a complaint or an inspection; the responses, if any, to the November 5 demands.

What is being asked

A pattern-or-practice investigation by the DOJ Civil Rights Division under the Civil Rights of Institutionalized Persons Act against findings F-I1 through F-I6; review by Idaho POST and the Idaho Attorney General; an independent medical evaluation of the detainee and the release of his complete unredacted medical and administrative records; and judicial oversight in the District of Idaho — up to a special master and closure evaluation, on the FCI Dublin model (FC-003) — if the jail cannot be brought to standard. The filings themselves demand an immediate federal investigation, protection, restored medical access, and the release of complete unredacted medical and administrative records. The founder’s demand — a demand, not a maybe, and the correction falls on them: “I want all these bastards. Every single one fired and Brady listed.” Fired and Brady-listed, every single one of them, now. At this facility that names Sheriff Doug Ulmer, Lt. Richard Knutson, and Sgt. Steven Gabor — the jail staff named in these filings. It is his demand, not a finding.

What is not established

The October 9 threat, the disability harassment, the mail routing, the records block, the failure to investigate, and the retaliatory purpose of the transfer are all single-source — the mother’s and her son’s account. No jail video, medical record, mail directive, POA document, or complaint log is in hand. No DOJ, FBI, or Idaho State Bar response has been received; a DOJ record number is a submission receipt, not an opened investigation, and a Bar grievance is not a finding of misconduct. The three tort claims prepared per the filing log have not been received. The filing log framed DOJ complaint 866863 as the son versus the Sheriff, county officials, and Deputy Prosecutor Matt Jessup; the printout is filed in the mother’s name against the Sheriff and Lt. Knutson, and Jessup’s name does not appear in the pages read — the substance matches, the framing differs, and the discrepancy is flagged, not resolved. The criminal case against the mother (CR25-26-1001) and the DOJ number 746168-NMN are referenced in her public posts and have not been independently verified. Two investigators’ material is pending and not relied on. This entry makes no finding of crime, misconduct, or liability. Liability is a question for an attorney, and any claim turns on proof.

For families

General information only — not legal advice. Formal channels: the jail’s grievance process at every level, appealing each denial — exhaustion matters for prisoners under 42 U.S.C. 1997e; the Idaho County Sheriff’s Office; the Idaho County Board of Commissioners; Idaho POST; the Idaho Attorney General; the DOJ Civil Rights Division; Disability Rights Idaho; congressional casework through the Idaho delegation. Document every step — outside reviewers ask for it first.
